STYLE GUIDANCE:
- Cinematic visual metaphor illustration that makes abstract financial data visceral and emotional — NOT literal charts or graphs.
- Translate data stories into physical metaphors: a skyscraper shaped like a bar chart shattering for market crashes, a country folding origami-style into a briefcase for recession, a mountain-sized printing press for QE, a golden palace floating above flooded streets for wealth inequality.
- Style: conceptual editorial illustration reminiscent of Der Spiegel or The Economist cover art — clean, intentional, muted but not childish.
- Color palette: deep navy (#0A1628) backgrounds and shadows, electric lime (#00FF88) for growth and positive reveals, crimson (#CC0000) for crashes and danger, gold (#FFD700) for wealth and money.
- Use familiar objects made strange — everyday items (grocery baskets, buildings, briefcases) with financial logic applied in surreal ways.
- Scale contrast is essential: tiny humans next to enormous financial forces makes incomprehensible numbers feel real.
- Color as narrative: warm-to-cold transitions carry stories (prosperity gold to crisis grey). A single image should feel like a scene from a story, not a data display.
- Composition: dramatic wide shots or low angles for maximum impact, 9:16 vertical, cinematic foreshortening.
- Painterly quality with visible brushstroke texture — gouache or thick ink feel, not smooth digital render.
- Shallow depth of field effect where appropriate — sharp subject, softer background for focus.

REMINDER: Deliver strictly visual storytelling. No words, numbers, lettering, data labels, chart axes, or readable elements anywhere. Pure visual atmosphere — captions and data overlays are added separately in video production.
